# Synthetic worked example

**Entirely fictional training example.** The names, sites, records, and timestamps below are invented. The reserved `.example.com` domain is used only as a placeholder. Nothing here describes a real business or person.

## Question and scope

**Question:** Do `shop-a.example.com` and `shop-b.example.com` appear to share an operator, based on the fictional public-page observations below?

**Scope:** Two fictional storefront home pages and their visible footer content, observed during a made-up one-day window. No contact, authentication, scanning, or outside inference is part of this example.

## Observation log

| ID | Observation (directly seen in this fictional scenario) | Source and time | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| O-1 | A fictional page at `shop-a.example.com` displays a footer link labeled “Returns” pointing to `shop-b.example.com/returns` | Fictional capture S-1, 2026-01-12 10:00 UTC | A visible link does not identify who created or controls either page |
| O-2 | Both fictional home pages display the same distinctive typo in a product category heading | Fictional captures S-1 and S-2, 2026-01-12 10:00 UTC | Could result from copied text, a shared template, or a common content vendor |
| O-3 | The example records say the two captures were made independently from each other | Fictional capture notes S-1 and S-2 | Independent capture does not make the underlying page content independent evidence |

## Inference

The cross-site footer link is a direct indication that one page points to the other at the recorded time. The matching typo is a content similarity. Neither observation identifies the operator or proves that the link was authorized, current beyond the capture time, or created by the same party.

The two page observations share one underlying content context. They should not be counted as two independent confirmations of common control simply because there are two URLs.

## Competing hypotheses

- **H1:** The two storefronts are operated by the same party
- **H2:** One site links to an unrelated or copied page, perhaps through a stale link
- **H3:** A shared template or content provider accounts for the repeated typo
- **H4:** The pages were altered or misrepresented in the fictional capture scenario

The example provides no evidence that would distinguish H1 from H2 or H3. The shared typo is weak evidence of linkage. The footer link merits attention, but by itself it does not establish control or wrongdoing.

## Confidence and disposition

- **Claim: the recorded page at shop-a linked to the recorded returns page at shop-b:** High confidence within this fictional scenario, because it is directly stated in the invented capture record. This says nothing about current live state.
- **Claim: the two pages share an operator:** Low confidence; unresolved alternatives remain and no independent operator evidence is supplied.
- **Claim: either storefront is fraudulent:** No supported conclusion.

**Human disposition:** Keep both fictional entities at “unresolved lead” for this example. Do not assign guilt, transfer a negative classification, contact anyone, or publish an allegation. A reviewer may identify a safe, authoritative source that could distinguish the hypotheses, but this scenario contains no such source.

## Example report sentence

“In this synthetic example, one captured storefront page links to the other and both display the same typo. Those observations may justify further review, but they do not establish common control or fraud; a shared template, copied content, or a stale link remain plausible.”

## Provenance fields demonstrated

A real workflow would preserve source identifier, original reference, observation time and zone, capture method, content description or excerpt, analyst, integrity notes, claim supported, alternatives, reviewer, and disposition date. This example invents those fields and includes no real source URLs or indicators.
